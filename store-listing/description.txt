Chrome's Memory Saver puts background tabs to sleep, but not the tab that's selected in each window. If you keep a lot of windows open, that tab stays loaded in every one of them, even in windows you haven't looked at for hours.

Window Parker parks the windows you aren't using. When a window has been idle for 15 minutes (you can change this), it selects a small parking page at the end of that window, so your tab becomes a background tab that Chrome can put to sleep. When you go back to the window and stay for a couple of seconds, your tab is selected again.

The parking page shows the icon, title and website of the tab you left, so you can tell parked windows apart. Window Parker never moves windows or changes tab order, pins or groups. Swiping quickly through desktops doesn't wake the windows you pass.

• By default Chrome decides when a parked window's tabs sleep. You can have them unloaded straight away instead.
• A window stays awake if its selected tab is pinned, playing audio, protected, or on a site you've excluded.
• Parking pauses while a download is in progress.
• The toolbar popup shows how many windows are parked and how many tabs are asleep. You can protect the current tab or pause parking from there, and clear parked or protected tabs after a confirmation.
• Right-click a web page to protect the tab, exclude the site or pause parking.
• You can sync chosen settings between your computers with Chrome sync.
• Light and dark themes, or follow the system setting.

Privacy: no account, server, analytics or ads, and nothing is sent to the developer. Sync is off unless you turn it on for a setting.

About Chrome's install warnings: "Read your browsing history" is how Chrome describes access to your open tabs' addresses and titles. Window Parker uses those for site exclusions and to bring back the right tab, and it can't read your browsing history. "Manage your downloads" is only used to check whether a download is in progress.

Designed for macOS desktops (Spaces) and tested on macOS. Needs Chrome 120 or newer. Chrome doesn't tell extensions about every call, upload or unsaved edit, so add sites where that matters to your exclusions.
